# Niche Research — November 2026

## Your constraints
- Interests: open, so pick based on demand
- Time: **under 5 hours/week** (about 1 solid article a week)
- Budget: **$100–500** for year one
- Country: **United States** (W-9, FTC disclosure rules)

## What the 2026 research says (shapes every choice below)
- **Google's 2026 updates hit affiliate sites hard**, especially thin "top 10"
  roundups by unnamed authors. One study of 400+ sites found affiliate review
  sites lost the most traffic, about 58% on average.
- **What still ranks:** first-hand testing and original photos/data, a named
  author, and depth on one narrow topic. Fewer, deeper articles beat many thin ones.
- **Heavy ad loads hurt recovery**, so stay light on display ads.
- **Amazon keeps cutting rates.** Most high-volume categories pay 1–4%, with more
  cuts reported in 2026. Use Amazon as a backup, not the main income.
- **Crowded categories to avoid:** cooking, fitness, finance, digital marketing, travel.
- **Best openings for small sites:** specialty products sold by retailers that run
  their own programs (often 10–30%), and software with recurring or flat payouts.

What this means for you: with 5 hours a week, every article has to come from your
own experience. So the niche needs to be something you can *actually use and test*
on a $100–500 budget.

## Shortlist

### Option A — Indoor / countertop hydroponic gardens (recommended)
- **Programs:** AeroGarden (reported 7.5–10%, 40-day cookie, via CJ), Gardyn
  (reported ~$100 per sale), Lettuce Grow (Impact), plus seed pods, grow lights
  and nutrients on Amazon.
- **Why it fits:** one starter unit costs ~$100–200, so hands-on testing fits the
  budget. You get months of content from one unit (setup, week-by-week growth,
  what grew well, troubleshooting). Very photogenic, which suits Pinterest, a
  low-time traffic channel. Evergreen, with a holiday-gift spike.
- **Risks:** a few big brands and gardening publishers rank for the broadest
  terms, so go narrow (beginner problems, specific plants, apartment growing).
- **Sample articles:** "AeroGarden Harvest after 90 days: honest review",
  "Why are my hydroponic basil leaves turning yellow", "Best plants to grow in an
  AeroGarden (ranked by what actually worked)".

### Option B — Software for one-person home-service businesses
- **Programs:** Jobber (PartnerStack; reported 20% recurring or $50–300 per sale),
  Housecall Pro (PartnerStack; reported up to $320 per qualified lead plus $180
  per enrollment), QuoteIQ (advertises 40% recurring for life).
- **Why it fits:** testing is free (free trials), and one sale can pay $100–300+.
- **Risks:** big software review sites (G2, Capterra) and the vendors' own blogs
  compete hard. You need a credible angle, such as running or having worked in a
  cleaning, lawn-care or handyman business. Without that, it's a hard niche to win.

### Option C — Beginner backyard beekeeping
- **Programs:** specialty retailers paying 10–15% (Basic Honey, Bee Inspired, and others).
- **Why it fits:** low competition and passionate buyers.
- **Risks:** you'd have to keep bees for real ($300–600+ to start, seasonal work,
  more than 5 hours a week in season). A poor fit for your time and budget.

## Recommendation
**Option A, indoor hydroponic gardening for beginners and apartment dwellers.**
It's the only option where the real testing Google now rewards fits under
5 hours a week and $500, the commissions beat Amazon's, and the topic suits
Pinterest, which works with little time.
