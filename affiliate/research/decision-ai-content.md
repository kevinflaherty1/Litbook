# Decision: Niche + How We Use AI Writing

## The decision
**Niche: indoor / countertop hydroponic gardening for beginners and apartment dwellers.**
**Author: a consistent pen name. AI writes the drafts; you supply real experience.**

## Why AI writing changes the plan (and why hydroponics still wins)

Three hard facts about publishing AI-written affiliate content in 2026:

1. **Google demotes mass-produced AI content that adds nothing new.** The 2026
   core updates rewarded "information gain" (original data, photos, testing) and
   hit thin AI roundups hardest. AI text alone puts you in the same pile as
   thousands of other AI sites.
2. **Fake experience is illegal, not just risky.** The FTC's 2024 rule on fake
   reviews explicitly covers AI-generated reviews that misrepresent experience.
   An AI article that says "I tested this for 3 months" when nobody did can bring
   fines. Affiliate networks also ban accounts for it.
3. **Your 5 hours a week becomes the moat.** If AI does the writing, your hours
   go into what AI can't fake: using one real product and taking photos and notes.

So the setup is: **you are the tester, AI is the writer, and I'm the editor and
researcher.** In software niches (Option B) every competitor can do the same
AI-plus-free-trial routine, and big review sites dominate. A real plant growing
on your counter is something AI-only competitors can't copy.

## The weekly workflow (about 4–5 hours)

| Time | Who | Task |
|---|---|---|
| 30 min | You | Check the garden, take 5–10 phone photos, jot rough notes or a voice memo (what grew, problems, surprises) |
| 15 min | You | Paste notes + photos into the session, pick next topic from the content plan |
| — | AI | Draft the article from your notes + manufacturer specs + research |
| 60–90 min | You | Read the draft, fix anything untrue, add one or two sentences in your own words |
| 30 min | You | Publish, add photos, make 2–3 Pinterest pins (AI writes pin text) |
| 30 min | You | Monthly-ish: check stats, apply to programs, refresh an old post |

## Content rules for AI drafts (non-negotiable)
- Only claim experience that actually happened, in your notes or photos.
- For products we haven't used, write "research-based comparison" and say so
  in the article ("we haven't tested the Gardyn; here's what the specs and
  owner reports show").
- Every factual claim (price, size, wattage) is checked against the
  manufacturer's page.
- No invented quotes, testimonials, star ratings or test numbers.
- Each article must include at least one thing a generic AI article wouldn't
  have: your photo, your result, your mistake, your measurement.
- The pen name is fine. Use it consistently, write a short honest About page
  ("a beginner documenting my first indoor garden"), and don't invent
  credentials like "certified horticulturist".

## Content types ranked by how well they suit AI drafting

| Type | AI suitability | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Troubleshooting how-tos ("why are my seedlings leggy") | High | Factual; your photos add proof |
| Beginner guides / explainers | High | Great for Pinterest and internal links |
| Growth journals / "week 6 update" | Medium | AI organizes your notes; the content is yours |
| Single-product reviews | Only for products you own | Built from your notes |
| "Best X" comparisons | Medium | Label clearly as research-based for untested products |

## Extra edge: small interactive tools
The repo already uses Next.js, so I can build simple tools that AI-only sites
won't have, like a "what can I grow under my light" picker or a harvest
schedule calculator. Tools earn links and Pinterest saves.

## The one purchase that unlocks everything
One beginner countertop unit (~$100–200). Buy it through a retailer, *not*
through your own affiliate link (self-referrals break most program rules).
Start it the week the site goes live so the growth journal starts on day one.

## Update: which unit to buy and the angle (researched Nov 2026)

**AeroGarden caution:** Scotts Miracle-Gro announced AeroGarden would close on
Jan 1, 2025. Some blogs say it relaunched in 2025, and stock was on clearance in
2026, but I couldn't confirm that with Scotts. **Don't build the site around
AeroGarden's affiliate program.** The confusion is still a content
*opportunity*: many people own AeroGardens and are searching for compatible
pods, replacement parts and alternatives.

**Broaden the niche slightly:** "indoor / countertop smart gardens", not strictly
hydroponics, so soil-pod systems like Click & Grow are covered too.

**Buy this unit: Click & Grow Smart Garden 9** (roughly $150–250 depending on
sales; the Smart Garden 3 at ~$100 is the backup if money is tight).
- Its affiliate program reportedly pays a 10% base rate (up to 25% private
  deals), with a 45-day cookie, via CJ.
- It has repeat purchases (pod subscriptions), which suits affiliates.
- Very beginner-friendly, so your beginner experience *is* the content.

**Programs to apply to later:** Click & Grow (CJ), Gardyn, Lettuce Grow (Impact),
Amazon Associates (pods, grow lights, the iDOO budget unit at ~$85–90).

**Content angles to own first:**
1. A "Smart Garden 9 growth journal" from your real unit (weekly posts)
2. Beginner troubleshooting (mold on pods, leggy seedlings, yellow leaves)
3. "AeroGarden alternatives" and "AeroGarden-compatible pods" (demand from owners)
4. Research-based comparisons, clearly labeled: Click & Grow vs Gardyn vs iDOO vs Lettuce Grow

**Competition reality:** CNN Underscored and several niche sites already rank for
broad terms like "best countertop garden". We won't fight for those early.
We'll win long-tail beginner questions and model-specific searches first.
